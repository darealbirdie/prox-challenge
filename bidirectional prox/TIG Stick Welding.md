# TIG / Stick Welding Specifications

## TIG Process Specifications

### Power Input Options
- **120 VAC / 60 Hz** (Standard)
- **240 VAC / 60 Hz** (High Power)

### Current Input at Output
| Voltage | 120V Setting | 240V Setting |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Current** | 20.6 A at 125 A output | 15.6 A at 175 A output |

### Welding Current Range
- **120V:** 10 A – 125 A
- **240V:** 10 A – 175 A

### Rated Duty Cycles
| Voltage | Rating |
|---------|--------|
| **120V** | 40% @ 125 A<br>100% @ 90 A |
| **240V** | 30% @ 175 A<br>100% @ 105 A |

## TIG Welding Process

### Materials Weldable with TIG
- Mild Steel
- Stainless Steel
- Aluminum (with AC capability)
- Other non-ferrous metals

### Advantages
- Precise control
- High quality welds
- No spatter
- Clean welds
- Suitable for thin materials

### Equipment Needed
- TIG Torch
- Tungsten electrodes
- Shielding gas (Argon/Helium mix)
- Filler rod (optional)
- Foot pedal or hand control (recommended)

---

## TIG Setup Guidelines

### Gas Selection
- **Pure Argon:** Most common, all materials
- **Argon-Helium Mix:** Better heat for thick aluminum
- Flow rate: 15-20 CFH typical

### Tungsten Selection
- **2% Thoriated:** DC, long life (radioactive)
- **Ceriated:** AC/DC, good for aluminum
- **Lanthanated:** AC/DC, versatile
- **Pure Tungsten:** AC aluminum only

### Polarity
- **DCEN (Straight):** Steel, stainless (DC negative on torch)
- **DCEP (Reverse):** Rarely used for TIG
- **AC:** Aluminum (alternating current)

## Stick Welding Process

### Covered Electrodes
- Flux coating provides shielding
- No external gas required
- Portable and versatile
- Works in wind

### Common Electrode Types
- **E6010:** Deep penetration, DC only, pipe
- **E6011:** AC/DC, general purpose
- **E6013:** Easy to use, AC/DC, sheet metal
- **E7018:** Low hydrogen, high strength, DC only

### Amperage Guidelines
- **1/8" electrode:** ~90-130 A
- **3/32" electrode:** ~60-90 A
- **1/16" electrode:** ~40-70 A

---

## Process Selection Guide

### Choose MIG When:
- High production needed
- Easy to learn
- Long welds
- Less cleanup required
- Semi-automatic process

### Choose TIG When:
- High quality critical welds
- Thin materials
- Precision required
- Visible welds (cosmetic)
- Full control needed

### Choose Stick When:
- Outdoor/windy conditions
- Portable operation
- Maintenance/repair
- Dirty or rusty materials
- No gas supply available

---

## Switching Between Processes

### Safety First
1. Turn OFF welder
2. Unplug from power
3. Allow to cool
4. Change torch/gun
5. Adjust settings
6. Check connections

### Setup Changes
- MIG → TIG: Change gun, adjust gas, set polarity
- TIG → Stick: Remove gas, change leads, adjust settings
- Stick → MIG: Add gas, change gun, adjust wire feed

---

## Control Panel Settings

### Process Selection
- MIG/Flux-Cored: Wire feed mode
- TIG: TIG torch connection, gas flow
- Stick: Electrode holder connection

### Common Settings Across Processes
- **Amperage:** Primary control
- **Voltage:** (MIG only) Arc length
- **Gas Flow:** (MIG/TIG only)
- **Wire Feed:** (MIG/Flux-cored only)

## Duty Cycle Comparison

### 120V Operation
| Process | Rating | Best For |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **MIG** | 40% @ 100A | Production |
| **TIG** | 40% @ 125A | Precision |
| **Stick** | ~40% @ 100A | Versatile |

### 240V Operation
| Process | Rating | Best For |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **MIG** | 25% @ 200A | Heavy duty |
| **TIG** | 30% @ 175A | Thick materials |
| **Stick** | ~30% @ 175A | High output |

## Material Thickness Guidelines

### MIG
- **Thin:** 24G+ (with care)
- **Light:** 18-22G (best)
- **Medium:** 14-18G (easy)
- **Thick:** Up to 3/8"+ (multiple passes)

### TIG
- **Thin:** 26G+ (possible)
- **Light:** 20-24G (excellent)
- **Medium:** 14-18G (good)
- **Thick:** Up to 1/4"+ (with skill)

### Stick
- **Light:** 14G+ (with skill)
- **Medium:** 10-14G (good)
- **Thick:** Up to 1/2"+ (multiple passes)
