# Owner's Manual - Key Technical Sections

## Specifications Summary

### MIG Welding (Page 7)

#### 120V Configuration
- **Power Input:** 120 VAC / 60 Hz
- **Current Input at Output:** 20.8 A at 100 A output
- **Welding Current Range:** 30 – 140 A
- **Rated Duty Cycles:**
  - 40% @ 100 A (can weld 4 min out of 10)
  - 100% @ 75 A (can weld continuously)
- **Maximum OCV:** 86 VDC

#### 240V Configuration
- **Power Input:** 240 VAC / 60 Hz
- **Current Input at Output:** 25.5 A at 200 A output
- **Welding Current Range:** 30 – 220 A
- **Rated Duty Cycles:**
  - 25% @ 200 A (can weld 2.5 min out of 10)
  - 100% @ 115 A (can weld continuously)
- **Maximum OCV:** 86 VDC

#### Weldable Materials - MIG
- Mild Steel: ✓
- Stainless Steel: ✓
- Aluminum: ✓ (with optional Spool Gun)

#### Wire Sizes - MIG
- Solid Core: 0.025" / 0.030" / 0.035"

## Page 6 - Grounding Information

### Grounding to Prevent Electric Shock and Death

**Critical Safety Check:**
> Check with a qualified electrician if you are in doubt as to whether the outlet is properly grounded.

### Grounding Requirements

1. **Do not use the welder if:**
   - Power cord is damaged
   - Plug is damaged
   - Outlet is not properly grounded

2. **If damaged:**
   - Have it repaired by a service facility
   - Do not attempt to use
   - Do not bypass grounding

3. **Proper grounding prevents:**
   - Electric shock
   - Death from electrical current
   - Equipment damage

### Ground Wire Connection Table (Page 6)

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Workpiece Ground Cable | Connects workpiece to ground |
| Torch Cable | Connects torch to welder |

## Page 29 - Duty Cycle Tables

### Understanding Duty Cycles

**What Duty Cycle Means:**
- Percentage of time welder can operate continuously
- Measured over 10-minute period
- 40% duty cycle = 4 minutes welding, 6 minutes cooling
- 100% duty cycle = continuous operation

### MIG Duty Cycles (Page 29)

## Important Notes

### Voltage Selection

**Use 120V when:**
- Standard household outlet available
- Light to medium duty work
- Thinner materials (< 1/4")
- Lower production requirements

**Use 240V when:**
- Heavy-duty work required
- Thicker materials (> 1/4")
- Higher production rates
- Maximum amperage needed

### Duty Cycle Management

**To prevent overheating:**
1. Monitor duty cycle ratings
2. Allow cooling between cycles
3. Don't exceed rated amperage
4. Ensure proper ventilation
5. Keep cooling fan unobstructed

### Material Selection

**For best results:**
- Match process to material
- Use correct wire/electrode
- Adjust parameters properly
- Clean base metal
- Maintain proper technique
